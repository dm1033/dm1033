## 4. LinkedIn posts (10) — longer-form

1. **The seconds-long scan.** I've spent 29 years in construction. Here's what surprised me most when I started reviewing CVs professionally: the experience is almost always there. The presentation almost never is. Recruiters scan fast. Software scans faster. A CV that buries a £40m highways scheme under "various civil projects" loses to a weaker candidate who wrote it properly. Your experience is the asset. Presentation is the access. (CTA: free checklist link)

2. **"Responsible for" is killing your CV.** Count how many of your bullets start with "Responsible for". Each one tells a recruiter what your job description said — not what you delivered. Swap them: action, scale, standard, outcome. "Coordinated temporary works on a £40m scheme — 25+ design briefs to BS 5975, zero NCRs." If it's true, write it like it happened.

3. **The TWC who couldn't be found.** A temporary works coordinator with 12 years' experience asked why recruiters never approached him. His LinkedIn headline: "Engineer at [Company]". The words "temporary works" appeared nowhere on his profile. Recruiters search words. If your specialism isn't written down, you're invisible. (CTA: LinkedIn service)

4. **Nobody can guarantee an ATS pass — run from anyone who says otherwise.** Screening systems differ by employer. Decisions involve humans. What you CAN control: standard headings, clear dates, honest keywords, project evidence, simple formatting. That's screening-readiness. The rest is the market.

5. **Site engineer → project manager: the evidence is probably already there.** Lookaheads you ran. Packages you coordinated. Variations you flagged. Progress meetings you covered. Most site engineers with 4+ years have PM evidence scattered through their week — and none of it on their CV. Reposition before you retrain.

6. **What Gulf employers read first.** UK CV: 2 pages, achievement-led, no photo. Gulf CV: project detail, named mega-projects, mobilisation status, visa position up front. Same career, different document. Sending the UK version to Dubai is why the phone is quiet. (CTA: International pack)

7. **Graduate engineers: you have more evidence than you think.** Final-year project = project entry. Group design work = coordination under deadline. Bar job = stakeholder management under pressure (genuinely). The skill isn't inventing experience — it's translating what you've already done into employer language.

8. **The cross-check.** Recruiter opens your CV. Recruiter opens your LinkedIn. Different job titles, different dates. Trust gone — in under a minute, through nobody's malice, just neglect. Align them this weekend. It costs nothing.

9. **Keyword stuffing is a confession.** White text, pasted adverts, skill lists you can't defend — modern systems flag it and interviews expose it. The honest version of keyword strategy: name your genuine experience in the industry's words. That's it. That's the whole trick.

10. **Why I started this business.** 29 years in civils, temporary works, HV and safety. I've watched brilliant site people lose roles to weaker candidates with better documents. The industry taught me what the evidence means; this business puts it on paper properly. CV reviews, rewrites, LinkedIn, coaching — built for construction. (CTA: site link)
